# Brand motion personalities

Choose a motion language from the logo and audience, then refine it for the hero. These are starting points, not fixed templates.

| Personality | Useful brand signals | Motion vocabulary | Restraint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cute and playful | Friendly shapes, warm palette, informal voice, welcoming audience | Soft bounce, squash and stretch, gentle float, blink, small wiggle | Keep the official mark legible; avoid constant movement and childish behavior for serious moments. |
| Cool and futuristic | Precise geometry, technical product, restrained palette | Geometric assembly, dimensional reveal, kinetic shapes, sparse particles | Avoid generic sci-fi glow, illegible fragments, and costly effects. |
| Premium and elegant | Refined typography, quiet palette, high-trust positioning | Gentle reveal, controlled light pass, slow graceful easing | Avoid spectacle, shine that distorts approved colors, and sluggish content entrance. |
| Bold and energetic | High contrast, expressive voice, action-focused audience | Rhythmic sequence, typography reveal, confident geometric transforms | Avoid aggressive flashes, excessive speed, and competing with the CTA. |

Other personalities may fit better: editorial, organic, architectural, reassuring, ceremonial, or deliberately still. Name the intended emotion and map each movement to a visible property of the actual brand. A bird mark may suggest a wing rhythm, but an abstract shape does not license fabricated anatomy or layers.

## Decision notes

Record: evidence from brand assets; audience and context; desired feeling; motion vocabulary; what must stay fixed; and why motion helps the hero. If those links are weak, recommend a static logo. Adapt intensity to page purpose: a campaign hero can carry more expression than a checkout, medical, or utility surface.
