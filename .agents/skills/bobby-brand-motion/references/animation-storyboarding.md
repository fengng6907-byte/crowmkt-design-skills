# Animation storyboarding and handoff

## Three-concept proposal

For each direction, state:

1. **Name and idea:** the brand evidence behind the movement and the intended feeling.
2. **Storyboard:** ordered beats with what the logo, supporting elements, headline, and CTA do; include the final static frame.
3. **Timing:** estimated total duration and main beat windows, including easing or movement character.
4. **Build path:** authentic assets required and recommended technology, with a reason tied to the stack.
5. **Adaptation:** mobile framing, touch behavior, reduced-motion and no-JavaScript fallback.
6. **Risks:** readability, complexity, download, rendering, or motion conflicts; note mitigations.

Recommend one concept based on brand fit, hierarchy, feasibility, accessibility, and performance. Do not present three cosmetic variations of the same effect. An AUDIT or PROPOSAL deliverable ends here unless authorized implementation follows.

## Animation specification

Convert the selected concept into a concise build specification: source asset and approved variant, frame or beat sequence, durations, easing values or named curves, trigger, one-time/replay behavior, interactions, layering and z-order, responsive adjustments, static final state, reduced-motion behavior, and acceptance checks. The headline and CTA should be available and usable while the logo animates. Avoid tying content visibility to a delayed animation completion callback.

## Illustrative brand applications

These examples describe decisions to explore. Confirm each real logo and guideline before designing or implementing.

### Crow Marketing

With the **existing official geometric bird logo**, explore three directions such as a restrained wing-inspired reveal of the intact mark, an assembly that follows genuine SVG groups, and a confident reveal through a mask. Recommend only after inspecting the actual bird file and hero. Do not draw a new bird, split flattened artwork into imaginary wings, or assume particular colors. Keep the mark recognizable and let the headline and CTA lead the composition.

### Tuition Centre

Use the **client's real logo**. Explore a gentle settle, welcoming reveal, or subtle guided movement that feels supportive to parents and students. Avoid childish bouncing, distracting loops, and motion that delays practical information. Confirm whether the identity is playful or formal before selecting.

### Technology Platform

Inspect the official logo and product voice. Explore precise assembly only if real vector structure supports it, an exact geometric wipe of the intact mark, or restrained dimensional motion. A fast, stable final frame often conveys more confidence than particles or large 3D effects. Avoid generic futuristic effects disconnected from the brand.
